Documents that read themselves.

Skilled people, retyping.Skilled people, retyping.

A typical renewal brings a fresh set of documents: the renewal offer, a new declarations page, perhaps an endorsement or a change in wording. Someone reads each one, finds the numbers that matter and keys them into the system, one field at a time. Commercial accounts multiply that with schedules of locations, vehicles and equipment.

It is careful, necessary work, and it is where errors creep in. A transposed digit in a dwelling limit or a missed sublimit can turn into an awkward conversation at claim time. Document intelligence takes on the reading and the keying, and leaves the checking to the person who knows the client.

From document to client file.From document to client file.

  1. 1

    The document arrives

    Upload a PDF or scan, forward it from email, or open it from the download inbox where insurer documents arrive through CSIO eDocs, where available.

  2. 2

    Tavio works out what it is

    It identifies the document type, the insurer and the policy, and suggests the client and policy it belongs to.

  3. 3

    Details are extracted

    Insured names, policy number, term, coverages, limits, deductibles, premium and endorsements are pulled into the matching fields.

  4. 4

    A person reviews side by side

    Each value is shown next to its source line. Uncertain values are flagged and must be checked before saving. Changes from the expiring policy are highlighted.

  5. 5

    Saved and recorded

    Confirmed values are saved to the policy, the original document is filed on the client record, and the review is written to the audit trail.

Safeguards built in.Safeguards built in.

  • Every extracted value is shown next to the line and page it came from
  • Values the AI is unsure about are flagged and can't be saved until a person checks them
  • Nothing is written to the policy until someone confirms it
  • The original document is always kept on the client file
  • Extraction respects the reviewer's permissions, like everything else in Tavio
  • Each review is recorded: who checked it, what was accepted and what was corrected

About accuracy

Accuracy depends on the quality and layout of each document. Clean, digital PDFs read best, while faded scans and handwritten notes need more checking. That's why every value is shown with its source and a person confirms the data before it is saved.

FAQ

Document intelligence questions.Document intelligence questions.

Does it work with any insurer's documents?

It is designed to read documents from many insurers and layouts, but results vary with the format and quality of each document. The document types we support for your brokerage are confirmed during onboarding.

Does it replace CSIO eDocs?

No. Where insurers deliver documents electronically using CSIO standards, Tavio files them to the right client, and document intelligence helps with the reading. It also handles documents that arrive by email or on paper. Insurer availability varies by insurer and is confirmed during onboarding.

What happens to the original document?

It is filed on the client record alongside the extracted data, so anyone can check the source later.

Can it read handwritten applications?

Printed and digital documents give the best results. Handwriting and poor scans are more likely to be flagged as uncertain, and a person should expect to check more values.

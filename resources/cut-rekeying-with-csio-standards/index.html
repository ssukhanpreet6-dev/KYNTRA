Cutting rekeying with CSIO standards

What CSIO standards are, how policy downloads and eDocs reduce double entry, and the habits that help a brokerage get the most from insurer connectivity.

  • Operations
  • Insurer connectivity
  • CSIO

The cost of typing it twice

Rekeying is the quiet tax on every brokerage. A CSR takes an address change from a client, enters it in the broker management system, then signs in to an insurer portal and types it again. The insurer issues a revised declarations page, and someone saves the PDF to the client file by hand. Each step is small. Across a book of thousands of policies and several insurers, it adds up to hours every week, along with the errors that come from typing the same thing twice.

Much of this work has a standard answer in Canada. It's worth understanding what the standards do, where they help most and what a brokerage needs to do to get the benefit.

What CSIO is

CSIO, the Centre for Study of Insurance Operations, is a not-for-profit organization that develops data and technology standards for the Canadian property and casualty insurance industry. Its standards give insurers, brokers and software providers a common way to describe clients, policies, coverages, transactions and documents, so information can move between systems without being re-entered.

Standards matter because they let one broker management system work with many insurers in a consistent way, rather than every connection being built from scratch.

Where standards remove rekeying

Policy and claims downloads

With downloads, insurers send policy data electronically to the broker management system: new business, renewals, endorsements and cancellations, and in some cases claims information. Instead of typing the insurer's changes back into the client file, the system updates the policy record and flags anything that needs a person to look at it.

Electronic documents

CSIO's eDocs standard covers delivering policy documents, such as declarations pages, renewal documents and liability cards, electronically into the broker's system, attached to the right client and policy. No more downloading a PDF from a portal and filing it by hand.

Quoting and transactions

Standards also support sending risk information to insurers for rating and bringing quotes back, and increasingly support transactions such as policy changes. Information captured once at the brokerage can reach the insurer without being retyped into a portal.

Availability varies

Which transactions and documents are available depends on each insurer's own connectivity and on the lines of business involved. Always confirm what an insurer supports before planning a workflow around it.

Getting the benefit: five habits

Connectivity is only half the job. Brokerages that see the biggest reduction in rekeying tend to share a few habits.

  1. Keep client records clean. Downloads match against existing clients and policies. Duplicate client records, inconsistent names and outdated policy numbers create exceptions someone has to resolve by hand.
  2. Work the exception queue every day. Some downloads won't match, or will contain changes that need review. A short daily check stops the backlog from becoming a month-end clean-up.
  3. Set up every insurer that offers it. Downloads and eDocs are usually enabled insurer by insurer. Keep a simple list of which insurers are set up for which lines and document types.
  4. Retire the manual steps. Once a document type arrives electronically, stop saving it by hand. Old habits linger, and files end up with duplicates.
  5. Measure what's left. Ask the team to log the tasks they still rekey for two weeks. The list shows which insurers, lines and transactions to tackle next.

What still needs a person

Standards remove typing. They don't remove judgement. Someone still needs to review an endorsement that changes coverage, look closely at a renewal with a large premium change and decide what to tell the client. Good connectivity frees time for exactly that work. It also creates a better record: when policy data comes from the insurer, the brokerage and the insurer are working from the same information, which makes discrepancies easier to spot and resolve.

Questions to ask your software provider

  • Which CSIO standards does the system support today, and which are planned?
  • How are downloaded changes matched to clients and policies, and how are exceptions shown and resolved?
  • Are eDocs filed to the right policy automatically, and can staff see which documents arrived electronically?
  • Can information entered once for a quote be reused when the policy is issued or changed?
  • How is availability confirmed for our specific insurers and lines of business?

Glossary

Insurance terms, explained.Insurance terms, explained.

71 brokerage and insurance terms in plain language.

Browse the glossary
Abeyance
A dated follow-up reminder attached to a client, policy or claim, so that something gets done on time: chasing a signed form, reviewing a renewal, confirming an endorsement with the insurer. Abeyances keep work from depending on anyone's memory, and they need to be reassigned when someone is away so nothing slips.
Account rounding
Adding the other policies a client needs to an existing relationship, such as auto for a home client, a personal umbrella, or coverage for a home-based business. Rounding an account closes coverage gaps, and brokers often find that clients with several policies are more likely to stay.
Actual cash value (ACV)
A basis of claim settlement that pays the cost to replace damaged property minus depreciation for age and wear. ACV settlements are usually lower than replacement cost settlements for the same loss. The policy sets out which basis applies to which property.
Additional insured
A person or organization added to someone else's policy so they have protection under it, usually for liability arising from the named insured's work or premises. Common in commercial lines, where contracts and leases often require a contractor or tenant to add the other party as an additional insured.
Adjuster
The person who investigates a claim, assesses the damage and the coverage, and recommends or settles payment. Adjusters may be employed by the insurer or work for an independent adjusting firm the insurer hires.
Agency bill (broker bill)
A billing arrangement where the brokerage invoices the client, collects the premium and pays the insurer, usually net of the brokerage's commission and according to the insurer's statement. In Canada it's often called broker bill. Premiums collected are generally held in trust until they're paid to the insurer.
